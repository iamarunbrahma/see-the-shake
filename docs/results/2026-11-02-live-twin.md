## Findings

- **SAM 3.1 masks.** "washing machine" reaches IoU 0.981 to 0.982 against the renderer's mask. Spark's door phrases
  ("circular front door", "round glass door") reach 0.955 to 0.962. "front control panel" found nothing in 3 of 4
  runs. It is reported as missing, and the run continues on the other parts. The bare phrase "door" finds nothing
  on the twin (tried on the 960x540 test clip), while descriptive phrases like "round front door" do.
- **Misalignment: the rules say looseness.** This also happens with the renderer's exact masks on this 6 s, full
  resolution clip. A 4x of about 0.005 px (18 to 20x the noise floor, not injected by the twin) makes 3 harmonics
  (2x, 3x, 4x). The looseness rule fires before the misalignment rule, even with 2x/1x at 3.0. Spark said
  misalignment, and explained that 2x dominates and there is no 0.5x. Verification capped its confidence at low,
  and the card shows both verdicts.
- **Running-speed range.** Spark gives wide ranges such as 30 to 1600 rpm. Code clamps them to the measurable band
  (126 to 7140 rpm at 240 fps), and the strongest peak in the range was the true 1x every time (998 rpm, true
  1000). The real-time preview aliases the drum's rotation, so Spark is told not to judge speed from it.
- **Cost.** $0.059 to $0.070 per full run. SAM costs $0.012 per phrase (60 frames billed), Spark $0.010 to $0.024
  per call.

### Findings

- **SAM 3.1 is fine at 720p.** IoU is 0.982 to 0.984 for "washing machine" and 0.973 to 0.975 for the door.
  SAM missed no phrase.
- **The new plan prompt stops paying for invisible parts.** Spark asked for two parts per run (the machine and
  its door) and no control panel. SAM costs $0.024 per run instead of $0.036.
- **Rule verdicts are right on all four faults, and Spark agrees each time.** Misalignment is right here only
  because the higher noise floor hides the spurious 4x that broke it at 1080p. The harmonic fix (fbeb431, on the
  other branch) is what makes it right on purpose.
- **Cost:** $0.206 for the four runs, $0.046 to $0.058 each.

### Would break on a real clip

1. **Compression artifacts inflate velocity, and the alert level depends on it.** The phone encoding adds peaks
   that are not machine motion: a comb at multiples of 20 Hz in y (20, 40, 60, 80 Hz, likely the encoder's
   frame-type cadence at 240 fps / 12) and mixing products in x (63, 77, 97 Hz). Velocity weights each peak by
   its frequency, so these carry about 70% of the healthy run's velocity squared: 6.1 mm/s against an alert
   level of 7.0. A real healthy machine filmed this way could be reported as a fault from compression alone.
   Possible fixes (in `measure.py` / `fingerprint.py`): compute velocity from running-speed orders only, or label
   peaks on an encoder cadence comb the way light flicker is labelled, using the background part to find it.
2. **Small motions read low.** Default encoder tuning keeps 64% of the healthy 1x (0.03 mm) and 82 to 103% of
   the larger ones. The twin's 20% amplitude target holds for the near-lossless encoding, not for phone files.
   Real-clip amplitudes should be reported as lower bounds.
3. **The noise floor doubles** (about 0.0007 mm here). The sub-pixel tracking still separates every fault, but
   the margin is thinner on the real 720p clips, where the machine also spans fewer pixels than in the twin.
4. **Rotation metadata.** Phones save portrait clips as landscape plus a rotation tag. ffmpeg (the SAM sub-clip
   and Spark preview) and OpenCV (measurement) both apply it, so masks and frames agree; a test covers this.
   ffprobe's width and height are the stored, unrotated size, so a mask canvas built from `ClipInfo` would be
   transposed. `segment_parts` now takes its shape from the decoded sub-clip and checks it against the frame
   size SAM reports, failing with a clear message on a mismatch.
5. **Unknowns that only Arun's clips can settle:** the Redmi's real bitrate and its hardware encoder's cadence
   (24 Mbit/s and x264 are assumptions), and whether MIUI saves the clip at 240 fps or slowed to 30 fps (ingest's
   capture-fps override covers the second case).

- **Verdicts:** the rules are right on all eight runs, and Spark agrees with them every time.
- **Masks:** SAM 3.1 IoU is 0.977 to 0.984 for the machine and 0.953 to 0.980 for the door. In the two

  sub-clip, "washing machine front glass door" then reaches IoU 0.951.
- **Time:** every 1080p run is over 3 minutes. About 70 s of each is the first-time twin render, which is not in
  the step times. Measure (104 to 110 s) and magnify (52 to 54 s) take most of the rest, and the other session
  is speeding those up. The 720p and 960x540 runs take 2 to 2.5 minutes. The API steps take 17 to 22 s (plan),
  13 to 23 s (segment) and 18 to 34 s (diagnose).

  reads 466 px only because this synthetic rotation lays the machine on its side; a real portrait clip
  displays upright.

### Spark and the magnified clip

tokens (about $0.007), and in 3 of 4 runs more reasoning: a diagnosis costs $0.020 to $0.031 with the clip and
$0.012 to $0.019 without. At first Spark's explanations never mentioned the video, so the diagnosis now also asks
for `visual_observation` (which part moves most and its dominant direction, from the magnified half), and code
checks it against the motion measured inside the magnification band (0.8x to 1.25x running speed):

### Would break on a real clip (from these runs)

- **Compression artifacts.** On the live upload of the healthy phone-like clip, velocity is 6.0 mm/s against the
  7.0 alert. The features list harmonics 5x and 6x, and Spark cites 83 Hz and 117 Hz peaks labelled 5x and 7x,
  which are encoder artifacts (see the phone-like section). The verdict is still right, but with little margin.
- **Part phrases vary from run to run.** The retry covers the misses seen here.
